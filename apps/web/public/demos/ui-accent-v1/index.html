One accent — and what it means for the swipe page

The rule in the post you sent: two colours that get along, one that argues, and the one that argues goes only on what you press. Percho's paper pages already wear its first palette (cream, near-black, burnt orange). What differs is the discipline: the orange is on headings, links, ranks and charts, and the app has a second arguing colour — the forest green of the tab bar, the Map button and the cards.

Three questions below, each one a choice. Nothing here is built. Marks that carry a meaning of their own — ✓ good, ! watch, ✕ not included, the green meters — keep their colour in every option.

1 · Section headings: amber or grey

Amber then means two things on these pages instead of three: something to press, or the finding itself.

NowHeadings, links and dots all amber.

Grey headings suggestedThe amber left is “Change my mind” and the dots — both pressable.

What changes

  • The uppercase section headings on You, the city page and the community page, and the small headings over the take on side by side and on the review form.
  • Small: a colour on a handful of headings, nothing moves.
  • Cost: amber capital headings are part of the look these pages have had since the first design. This is the quieter version of it.

 Ranks, the community's line, links and dots take the feed's green.

C · green everywhere

  • The direction your own later designs took: the card redline (“forest green is the only accent”), the feed header and the home detail page are all green. Amber is what is left of the first spec on pages not redrawn since.
  • The feed, tab bar and cards do not change. The pages behind them do.
  • ✓ good / ! watch reads as a clean pair again, because orange is no longer also the accent.
  • Cost: green says “good”. A rank drawn in green reads as praise even when it is #400 of 443. Orange was neutral about that.
  • Green on the paper is 5.9 to 1 (orange 4.6), so small green links read better.

3 · The swipe card: one button

On the card four things wear the same white pill: the place caption, the sound / share / save capsule, the COMMUNITY tag and Explore. Only Explore is the thing to press next. Shown in today's colours.

…and Explore takes the accentThe post's rule to the letter. But the dark green sits on the film's dark foot and reads weaker than the white.

Also on this page, not drawn

  • The name is on the screen twice — 36pt in the header and 27pt on the card. The two largest lines of text say the same word. Each was decided on its own; together one of them could carry something else, or step down.
  • Map is the only coloured control above the card, and it is the secondary action. With the labels stepped back, Explore and Map stop competing; with Explore in the accent, Explore clearly leads.
  • The white capsule in the card's corner was your call (“keep the sound and saved button on the top right to be consistent with listing”), and so was the COMMUNITY tag over the name. The middle option changes how both are dressed, not where they are.
