Percho competitive market report

Percho sits at the intersection of four markets: AI-generated real-estate video, automated listing-marketing suites, multilingual agent marketing, and buyer-facing neighborhood discovery. Most competitors cover only one or two of them.

Executive summary

The closest broad product competitor is ListingAI, because it combines listing videos, AI copy, social campaigns, listing websites, market data and an agent workspace in one suite. The most focused video competitors are Reel-E, Consul, Tour Estate AI, VideoEstate.ai and BetterSpace. Matterport competes for virtual-tour budget but delivers spatial exploration rather than narrative community storytelling.

The larger strategic threat comes from the portals:

  • Zillow owns enormous buyer reach and increasingly proprietary immersive media.
  • Homes.com is already combining neighborhood information, community videos, Matterport tours, retargeting and multilingual conversational AI.
  • Realtor.com is making lifestyle and neighborhood reasoning central to AI search.
  • Redfin now supports multilingual conversational discovery and can suggest neighborhoods from stated buyer preferences.

However, none of the reviewed U.S. agent tools appears to make a locally grounded, multilingual neighborhood film — distributed through both U.S. and Asian social channels — the primary listing experience. That remains Percho's clearest defensible opening.

1 · Direct competitors — AI listing video

ListingAI

Video + copy + listing sites + social · Agent

Very high

What it offers

The broadest direct comparison. It turns one property record into MLS-ready descriptions with Fair Housing checks, listing videos built from photographs, virtual staging and photo edits, single-property websites, coordinated Posts / Stories / Reels with captions, social scheduling to Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn, CMA and market reports, seller-performance reports, and agent websites with QR/NFC lead tracking.

Its public site says it operates in more than 20 languages. Pricing starts with a free listing; paid plans are roughly $19/mo Essential, $36 Professional and $150 Expert, with annual discounts.

How Percho differs

ListingAI is optimized around the listing lifecycle and agent productivity. Its videos remain property-tour assets generated from listing photos. Percho differentiates by making the surrounding community the narrative lead and presenting the result as a buyer-discovery experience.

ListingAI is a real multilingual threat at the content-generation level. Its public materials do not, however, demonstrate culturally adapted distribution to Rednote or WeChat, or a specialized workflow for Spanish-, Chinese-, Vietnamese- and Korean-speaking U.S. buyers.

Assessment: Closest feature-suite competitor; the strongest benchmark for dashboard, copy, microsites and campaign completeness.

Sources: features · pricing · language availability

Reel-E

AI photo-to-video · Agent, team, media professional

High

What it offers

Turns listing photos into cinematic property videos using simulated camera motion, licensed music, branding and multiple aspect ratios. Every property project includes horizontal, vertical, square, branded and unbranded formats, plus a listing website. Generation is advertised at roughly two minutes.

  • Essential — $59/mo, three listings ($44 billed annually)
  • Growth — $129/mo, ten listings ($97 annually)
  • Pro — $599/mo, fifty listings ($449 annually)
  • Enterprise — custom

How Percho differs

Reel-E animates the supplied property photography. Percho's core object is the community story around a property. Reel-E also does not publicly position itself as a buyer destination, a multilingual marketing system, or a channel-localization product.

Assessment: Strong production and price competitor, but not yet a neighborhood-discovery competitor.

Source: Reel-E pricing and inclusions

Consul

AI photo-to-video · Agent

High

What it offers

Imports a Zillow, Realtor.com or MLS link — or accepts uploaded photos — and generates an AI-planned property tour with motion, virtual twilight, text and licensed music. Users can alter shots, text, music and format.

  • First 30-second watermarked video — free
  • Starter — $39/mo, about two videos
  • Agent — $99/mo, about five
  • Pro — $249/mo, about sixteen

How Percho differs

Consul sells convenience: existing listing assets in, conventional house-tour video out. It lacks the broader community-media proposition, the buyer-facing app, the multilingual copy suite and the Asian-platform workflow.

Assessment: A simple, easy-to-understand substitute when an agent thinks the job is merely "make my photos into a video."

Source: Consul product and pricing

Tour Estate AI

AI walkthrough / video · Agent, photographer, small agency

High

What it offers

Converts listing photos into cinematic room-to-room walkthroughs with real-estate-specific image motion, AI-selected sequencing, editable narration, music and virtual hosts, horizontal and vertical social formats, and imports from Zillow and Airbnb. It claims a roughly five-minute workflow and advertises plans from $29/mo across Free, Starter, Studio and custom Enterprise tiers, though exact live tier prices vary by country and billing period.

How Percho differs

Tour Estate AI focuses on interiors and room layout. Percho's differentiation is exterior context: culture, local routines, places and neighborhood identity. It also does not present an end-to-end multilingual listing and community-marketing destination.

Assessment: Important low-cost substitute for video, but a different storytelling frame.

Sources: overview · pricing explanation

BetterSpace

Video + staging + social workflow · Agent, team, agency

Medium-high

What it offers

Combines AI listing videos, virtual staging, AI avatar presenters and lifestyle actors, listing sync, social scheduling on team plans, Claude and ChatGPT integrations, and shared team workspaces. Pricing runs from a continuing free tier with two videos a month to $29 Lite, $49 Starter, $89 Growth and $159 Pro. Enterprise plans add brokerage administration and larger credit pools.

How Percho differs

BetterSpace increasingly resembles an AI media studio. Its differentiation is breadth of synthetic production, not proprietary neighborhood knowledge or culturally localized buyer acquisition. Percho should avoid competing solely on image animation, because products such as BetterSpace can rapidly commoditize it.

Assessment: Medium-to-high threat, especially if Percho's videos can be mistaken for ordinary photo animation.

Source: BetterSpace pricing

VideoEstate.ai

Video + image editing + descriptions · Agent, agency

Medium

What it offers

Generates 4K horizontal and vertical videos, AI photo edits and listing descriptions. Credit-based plans: Free, Lite $23/mo, Starter $57, Standard $117, Premium $237, Enterprise custom.

How Percho differs

VideoEstate.ai bundles high-volume creative utilities but remains property-asset-first. It does not publicly present community film generation, buyer discovery, cultural adaptation, or specialized U.S. multilingual segments as the central product.

Assessment: Price and production competitor; limited strategic overlap beyond video and descriptions.

Source: VideoEstate.ai pricing

Matterport

3D digital twins · Agent, photographer, property operator, buyer

Medium

What it offers

Creates navigable 3D digital twins from phone, tablet or 360-camera captures: interactive 3D tours and measurements, AI defurnishing, automated galleries, video clips and guided highlights, Property Intelligence and MLS-ready reports, embedding and publishing to Google Street View and CoStar-owned portals, and marketing analytics. A free tier supports one active space; Starter through Business pricing depends on active-space volume and is shown dynamically; Enterprise is quote-based.

How Percho differs

Matterport answers "what is it like to move through this building?" Percho answers "what is it like to live here?" Those experiences can be complementary, although they compete for the same agent marketing budget.

Assessment: A budget competitor and potential integration partner rather than a pure head-to-head rival.

Sources: plans · real-estate capabilities

2 · Adjacent competitors — copy, syndication, distribution

Xara

MLS-triggered marketing automation · Agent, brokerage

High

Uses MLS events to generate entire marketing campaigns, including branded print materials, emails, listing posts and automated social content, and can auto-post agent content to Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn. Public pricing: $49/mo for an individual agent; $129/mo for five agents at brokerage level, with MLS sync, automated social management and email tools.

Percho difference: Xara automates collateral and publishing around the property. Percho can provide a much richer media centerpiece and multilingual buyer experience. Xara is nevertheless a strong benchmark for MLS-triggered automation and brokerage economics.

Source: Xara real-estate pricing

Back At You Social

MLS-to-social automation · Brokerage, agent

High

Turns MLS status changes into social posts across Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, X, TikTok and YouTube, branded listing videos, paid Meta and web campaigns, single-property landing pages, seller reports and postcards, Fair Housing-compliant targeting, and lead capture with CRM handoff. Pricing is sales-led rather than publicly posted.

Percho difference: Back At You has greater conventional distribution automation, but no visible Rednote/WeChat or Asian-language specialization, and its videos are branded listing-photo compilations rather than neighborhood documentary products.
Strategic implication: Percho should match or integrate with MLS-status triggers; otherwise agents may prefer a less differentiated product that requires no manual setup.

Source: Back At You Social

Agent Crate

Social content and automation · Agent

Medium

Provides real-estate content calendars, more than 500 templates, Reels, carousels, neighborhood-tour templates, automatic Instagram and Facebook publishing, CRM, websites and funnels. Plans: Create $59/mo, Automate $99, Grow $149, Branded $199.

Percho difference: Agent Crate helps agents maintain a social presence using templates and recurring content. Percho generates listing-specific, geographically grounded films and localized buyer journeys. Agent Crate's "neighborhood tour" templates indicate real market demand — but templates are not the same as automated, evidence-based community films.

Source: Agent Crate plans

Luxury Presence

Website, CRM, ads, AI content · Top agent, team

Medium-high

Combines high-end agent websites, IDX, AI CRM, lead scoring, CMAs, hyperlocal content, advertising, social management and branded apps. In 2026 it added weekly campaigns containing static posts, carousels and AI-generated video Reels based on an agent's listings and brand. Pricing is quote-based, includes a setup fee, and generally requires a 12-month agreement.

Percho difference: Luxury Presence sells a full premium-growth platform to top performers and teams. Percho is narrower and potentially far easier to adopt per listing, and its multilingual and neighborhood-film proposition is more buyer-specific.

3 · Big-portal threats

Zillow

Portal + proprietary rich media · Buyer and agent

Very high

Current activity

Zillow's primary immersive product is Showcase, which gives listings premium placement and AI-powered rich media, interactive floor plans, virtual tours, room-by-room layouts and agent branding. In 2025 Zillow launched SkyTour, an interactive drone-like exterior experience created from drone footage using Gaussian splatting, which can show the lot and the area immediately around a Showcase property. Zillow says Showcase is intentionally limited to no more than 10% of listings in a market.

Zillow also reports that Showcase and 3D-floor-plan technology are on nearly 10% of new for-sale listings, and describes that rich-media corpus as proprietary training data for future AI experiences. Natural-language search and an AI assistant are part of its broader strategy.

Threat to Percho

  • Enormous distribution advantage
  • Proprietary listing and consumer-behavior data
  • Ability to bundle immersive media into the dominant portal experience
  • Strong agent-facing proof metrics for views, saves, shares and sale outcomes

SkyTour moves beyond the walls of the house, but it is still largely parcel and exterior visualization — not a narrated neighborhood story or a culturally localized guide.

Sources: SkyTour announcement · Showcase adoption · 2026 AI strategy

Redfin

Multilingual AI search · Buyer

High

Current activity

Redfin launched multilingual conversational search in November 2025. Buyers can describe preferences in any language, refine them conversationally, and discover neighborhoods beyond the boundaries they initially considered. Redfin says early users viewed nearly twice as many listings and were 47% more likely to request a tour or service. It also operates Ask Redfin for questions about listing details, local conditions, schools and zoning. Redfin accepts listing and 3D-tour media supplied through participating MLSs, but no proprietary neighborhood-film production product was found.

Threat to Percho

Redfin directly weakens any broad claim that "multilingual neighborhood discovery is unserved." It can already interpret multilingual preferences and suggest suitable homes or neighborhoods at national scale.

Percho's distinction must therefore be emotionally compelling, shareable media and agent-owned lead capture — not simply multilingual search or neighborhood information.

Sources: conversational search · Ask Redfin

Realtor.com

AI lifestyle and neighborhood search · Buyer

High

Current activity

Realtor.com launched visual AI search in 2025, analyzing both listing language and photographs. In June 2026 it introduced RealAssist AI, built with Gemini, to reason about affordability, properties and neighborhoods. RealAssist can:

  • Compare homes and neighborhoods
  • Identify community character and investment
  • Map a buyer's commute and daily routine
  • Highlight parks and nearby businesses
  • Visualize streets after dark or in other seasons
  • Remember buyer preferences across sessions

Threat to Percho

Realtor.com is moving aggressively into the underlying job Percho addresses: helping buyers understand whether a location fits their life. Its experience is conversational, analytical and map-based rather than an agent-branded neighborhood film.

Treat Realtor.com as a likely future entrant into auto-generated neighborhood video, even though no such product was confirmed in the reviewed materials.

Sources: 2025 AI search · RealAssist AI

Homes.com

Neighborhood content and community video · Buyer, agent, builder

Very high

Current activity

The portal closest to Percho's neighborhood emphasis:

  • Neighborhood, school, condo and community pages
  • Professionally produced community videos for boosted new developments
  • Matterport 3D tours and floor plans
  • AI-powered retargeting video ads across the web, Facebook and Instagram
  • Priority placement on relevant neighborhood and school pages
  • "Your Listing, Your Lead" positioning that sends inquiries to the listing agent

Boost pricing depends on the property and is not publicly standardized. Homes AI adds an important multilingual threat: it compares listings using neighborhood dynamics, amenities, schools and pricing, and can converse in more than 50 languages while guiding users through Matterport tours.

Threat to Percho

Homes.com already owns most of the ingredients — neighborhood editorial content, community videos, agent-aligned lead routing, portal distribution, multilingual AI, and listing and retargeting infrastructure.

Its principal gap is that community films appear concentrated in paid new-construction packages rather than automatically produced for ordinary resale listings. It also does not publicly demonstrate culturally adapted Rednote/WeChat campaign creation.
Assessment: The most important portal competitor, and the clearest validation of Percho's neighborhood-first thesis.

Sources: Community Boost · listing Boost · Homes AI

4 · The multilingual and cross-cultural gap

What is already served

  • ListingAI supports content in more than 20 languages.
  • Redfin conversational search accepts any language.
  • Homes AI claims more than 50 languages.
  • Generic AI, video-dubbing and translation products can cheaply translate scripts.
  • Specialist agencies already market U.S. brands through Rednote and WeChat.

Translation alone is not defensible.

What remains weakly served

No reviewed U.S. listing-marketing platform publicly demonstrated all of the following in one workflow:

  1. Import a residential resale listing.
  2. Generate a neighborhood-led film rather than an interior-photo slideshow.
  3. Produce culturally natural Spanish, Chinese, Vietnamese and Korean variants.
  4. Adapt — not merely translate — the caption, length, tags, tone and call to action for each channel.
  5. Publish or package content for Rednote and WeChat as well as U.S. networks.
  6. Send buyers to a multilingual listing/community page.
  7. Attribute engagement and leads back to the listing agent.

Search results surfaced general Chinese-market agencies such as BIYA and overseas portals, but no comparable automated U.S. agent product. BIYA, for example, provides bilingual Rednote account management to U.S. brands — a service agency, not a listing-to-film SaaS workflow.

The strongest gap is cross-cultural real-estate distribution, not machine translation.

Source: BIYA

5 · Strategic opportunities for Percho

1 · Define a category competitors cannot easily blur

"AI real-estate video" is rapidly commoditizing. Percho should consistently describe its output as a neighborhood film, a community story, a buyer orientation film — a "live here" film rather than a "tour this house" video. The opening seconds should prove that distinction: streets, routines, transit, businesses, parks, culture and lived experience before interiors.

2 · Build cultural adaptation, not a translation toggle

For each supported buyer segment, adapt:

  • Narrative order and decision criteria
  • Terminology and units
  • Subtitle density and voice style
  • Platform-specific title, cover image, hashtags and duration
  • Calls to action and preferred contact channel
  • Community institutions and amenities relevant to that audience

This is a more credible moat than supporting a large raw language count.

3 · Own Rednote and WeChat operationally

Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok and YouTube distribution is well served by Back At You, Xara and similar products. Rednote and WeChat are the less crowded wedge. Percho should provide channel-aware export packages even where direct API publishing is unavailable:

  • Correct dimensions and duration
  • Chinese cover and title variants
  • Suggested tags and compliant disclosures
  • WeChat article, Moments and chat-sharing variants
  • QR codes and deep links to localized listing pages
  • Approval workflows for bilingual agents or reviewers

4 · Make provenance and factual safety visible

Neighborhood films create Fair Housing and accuracy risks. Percho should show agents sources for factual statements, last-updated dates, editable scripts and place selections, Fair Housing warnings, clear identification of AI-generated or altered visuals, and controls that prevent demographic steering or protected-class proxies.

A trustworthy neighborhood story is worth more than an aesthetically impressive but unverifiable one.

5 · Connect films to measurable agent outcomes

Competitors sell efficiency; portals sell reach. Percho should sell buyer intelligence — film completion rate, language selected, places or chapters replayed, shares by platform, listing-page conversion, tour requests and agent contacts, buyer interest by neighborhood theme, and seller-ready campaign reports. That makes the dashboard a retention product instead of merely a content library.

6 · Automate the listing lifecycle

To compete with ListingAI, Xara and Back At You, Percho should trigger campaigns from MLS events: coming soon, new listing, open house, price change, under contract, sold. The neighborhood film can stay stable while copy, calls to action and social cuts update automatically.

7 · Treat portals as both threats and distribution channels

Percho is unlikely to out-distribute Zillow or Homes.com. It can instead ensure every film ships with MLS-safe/unbranded and agent-branded variants, portal-compatible video files and links, embeddable players, vertical cuts, a reusable neighborhood-film library, and clear licensing for agents, brokerages and portals.

8 · Accumulate reusable neighborhood assets

The most promising structural moat is a verified media and knowledge library for U.S. neighborhoods. Each new listing should reuse and refresh local footage and place assets, geographic facts, transit and amenity modules, pronunciations and localized names, language-specific narration, seasonal variants, and engagement data showing what matters to different buyer cohorts.

This changes unit economics: the first film builds the neighborhood; subsequent listings become inexpensive, customized editions.

Overall conclusion

Percho has a credible open position, but only if it avoids being perceived as another photo-to-video generator.

The direct-video market is already crowded and price competitive. ListingAI and BetterSpace are expanding horizontally, while Back At You and Xara are stronger at automated agent distribution. At the portal level, Homes.com, Realtor.com, Redfin and Zillow are rapidly improving neighborhood reasoning, immersive media and multilingual search.

Percho's most defensible position: the agent-owned, multilingual "what it is like to live here" media layer for every U.S. listing.

The key differentiation is the combination of:

  • Neighborhood-film-first storytelling
  • Culturally adapted multilingual content
  • Rednote and WeChat readiness
  • Agent-owned landing pages and leads
  • Verifiable local information
  • Cross-channel engagement analytics
  • Reusable neighborhood-media infrastructure

Neither neighborhood storytelling nor multilingual support is unique by itself. The opportunity lies in operationalizing both — at resale-listing scale — and making the result easy for agents to distribute and measure.
