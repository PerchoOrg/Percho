“Up to 5” — what it costs, and how it can work

Selecting 5 is easy. Showing 5 is where it breaks: the compare table gives each home a column, and five columns on a 390 pt phone leaves about 57 pt each — narrower than the words that go in them. Below is the same table at 3 and at 5, drawn to scale.

3 homes — today

Fits. Every figure readable at a glance, all three visible at once.

5 homes — same table, squeezed

Breaks. “$3,912/mo” gets its tail cut off, addresses shrink to “1450 Ki…”, and the at-a-glance comparison — the entire point — is gone.

5 homes — label pinned, values scroll →

Works. The row label stays put and the columns scroll sideways, so you always know which figure you're reading. You see 3 at a time and swipe to the rest. This is the change “up to 5” actually requires.

Three things I need you to decide

  1. The filter is the segment chips you deleted on 7 Sep — “remove home and community filtering sub tabs”. I think it's genuinely different this time and worth doing: the chips were a permanent bar that ate a row of screen and forced you into one segment; this is an icon that costs nothing when unused and defaults to showing everything. But it is the same function, so you should say yes to it on purpose rather than have me slip it back in.
  2. Mixed selection has to be ruled on. Homes, neighbourhoods and areas have three different compare tables — there's no such thing as comparing a house against a county. In these mockups the first thing you tap sets the kind and the rest dim out. It's the honest rule, but it means a tap can be refused, so watch how it feels.
  3. 5 costs you the side-by-side glance (see above). My recommendation: allow 5, pin the label column, scroll the values — and note that the take at the top doesn't suffer at all; it reads all five and still gives you one answer. If you'd rather keep the clean 3-up table, cap it at 4.
